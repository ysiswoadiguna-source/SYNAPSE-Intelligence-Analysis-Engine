## What are the five analytic standards and nine tradecraft standards of ICD 203?

### Takeaway
ICD 203 Section D.6 lists five analytic standards (a–e); the fifth, "implements and exhibits Analytic Tradecraft Standards", is broken into nine tradecraft standards, D.6.e(1)–(9).

### Cited Findings
- Five Analytic Standards (D.6.a–e): (a) Objective; (b) Independent of political consideration; (c) Timely; (d) Based on all available sources of intelligence information; (e) Implements and exhibits Analytic Tradecraft Standards. — [ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)
- Nine Analytic Tradecraft Standards (D.6.e(1)–(9)), with the gist of each requirement:
  1. **Properly describes quality and credibility of underlying sources, data, and methodologies.** Uses the source descriptors in ICD 206; source summary statements are strongly encouraged.
  2. **Properly expresses and explains uncertainties associated with major analytic judgments.** Covers both likelihood and confidence and explains where the uncertainty comes from (see next section).
  3. **Properly distinguishes between underlying intelligence information and analysts' assumptions and judgments.** States key assumptions explicitly, explains what follows if they are wrong, and names indicators that would change judgments.
  4. **Incorporates analysis of alternatives.** Identifies and assesses plausible alternative hypotheses, especially when uncertainty is high or when low-probability events could have major impact, and names indicators bearing on them.
  5. **Demonstrates customer relevance and addresses implications.** Covers prospects, threats, and opportunities for action.
  6. **Uses clear and logical argumentation.** Puts a "clear main analytic message up front", supports judgments with information and reasoning, and acknowledges significant contrary information.
  7. **Explains change to or consistency of analytic judgments.** Says whether major judgments match or differ from earlier analysis and why, avoids boilerplate, and points out significant differences between IC elements.
  8. **Makes accurate judgments and assessments.** Does not dodge difficult judgments to "minimize the risk of being wrong", and states likelihood, timing, and nature clearly.
  9. **Incorporates effective visual information where appropriate.** Uses tables, charts, or images where they convey information better than text.
  — [ICD 203, FAS mirror](https://irp.fas.org/dni/icd/icd-203.pdf); [ICD 203, archive.dni.gov](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)
- The ODNI Analytic Ombuds safeguards adherence to the standards (D.4). Under E.2 it is a senior executive reporting directly to the DD/MI who works independently and confidentially. — [ICD 203, FAS mirror](https://irp.fas.org/dni/icd/icd-203.pdf)

### Inferences
- The short names in the assignment (sourcing, uncertainty, assumptions vs judgments, alternatives, customer relevance, logical argumentation, consistency/change, accurate judgments, visual information) match the nine official standards one to one, in the same order.

### Gaps
- Exact verbatim wording of each sub-paragraph was not reproduced: the fetch tool limits quotes to short phrases. The descriptions above are close paraphrases.
- The date of the first (training) amendment could not be recovered from the PDF text.

## What are the exact seven likelihood bands and ranges in ICD 203?

### Takeaway
ICD 203 D.6.e(2)(a) requires one of seven paired term sets, each tied to a probability range from 01–05% up to 95–99%. Analysts are strongly encouraged not to mix terms from different columns, and a product that does must carry a disclaimer that the terms mean the same probability.

### Cited Findings
- Likelihood table (ICD 203, 2015):

| Range | Column 1 | Column 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 01–05% | almost no chance | remote |
| 05–20% | very unlikely | highly improbable |
| 20–45% | unlikely | improbable (improbably) |
| 45–55% | roughly even chance | roughly even odds |
| 55–80% | likely | probable (probably) |
| 80–95% | very likely | highly probable |
| 95–99% | almost certain(ly) | nearly certain |

  — [ICD 203, archive.dni.gov](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf); the band-by-band pairing is corroborated by [secondary reproduction (wphillipmoore)](https://wphillipmoore.github.io/the-infrastructure-mindset/research/R0052-methodology-claims/2026-03-31-02/C009-icd203-probability-scale/assessment)
- The seven printed ranges are 01–05, 05–20, 20–45, 45–55, 55–80, 80–95 and 95–99%. The scale deliberately leaves out 0% and 100%. — [ICD 203, FAS mirror](https://irp.fas.org/dni/icd/icd-203.pdf)
- Products must use one of these term sets. Analysts are strongly encouraged not to mix terms from different columns, and mixing requires a disclaimer that the terms indicate the same probability. — [ICD 203, archive.dni.gov](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)
- Predecessor: the 2007 NIC guidance "What We Mean When We Say" used unquantified terms (almost certainly, probably/likely, even chance, unlikely, remote) and noted that "unlikely" does not mean an event will not happen. — [Wikipedia: Words of estimative probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_of_estimative_probability)

### Inferences
- The bands share their boundary values (05, 20, 45, 55, 80, 95). The directive does not say which band an exact boundary value falls in.

### Gaps
- Text extraction from both PDFs scrambled the two-column layout. The ranges are certain, but the term-to-range pairing rests on the archive.dni.gov extraction plus one secondary source. For the 45–55% band, one extraction gave "even odds" and the secondary source gave "roughly even odds". "Roughly even odds" fits the pattern of the table, but check the original PDF before quoting it.

## What guidance exists on confidence levels and their basis?

### Takeaway
ICD 203 treats likelihood (how probable an event is) and confidence (how sound the analytic basis is) as two separate dimensions. It forbids putting a confidence level and a likelihood term in the same sentence.

### Cited Findings
- Under Standard 2, products should explain the basis for uncertainty in major judgments. This covers both the likelihood of an event and the analyst's confidence in the judgment. Confidence rests on "the logic and evidentiary base that underpin it, including the quantity and quality of source material." Products should also note what causes the uncertainty and how it affects the analysis. — [ICD 203, FAS mirror](https://irp.fas.org/dni/icd/icd-203.pdf)
- D.6.e(2)(b): products that express confidence with a "confidence level" (for example "high confidence") "must not combine a confidence level and a degree of likelihood" in the same sentence. Likelihood refers to an event or development. The rule exists to avoid confusing the two. — [ICD 203, archive.dni.gov](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)
- UK comparison: PHIA uses Analytical Confidence Ratings (AnCRs) of High, Moderate or Low. They are assessed across three categories (Information Base, Analytical Rigour, Complexity & Volatility), and each comes with a statement on the source and effect of the remaining uncertainty and how confidence could be raised. Probability is how likely a statement is to be true; confidence is the soundness and stability of the evidence behind that probability, including how likely the judgement is to change. — [UK Gov: Explaining uncertainty in UK intelligence assessment](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)

### Inferences
- In practice, a compliant US product gives the likelihood in one sentence (for example "We assess X is likely…") and the confidence in a separate sentence, with the reason (for example "We have moderate confidence in this judgment because…").

### Gaps
- ICD 203 itself does not appear to define high, moderate and low as formal levels with criteria. That level of detail is in agency tradecraft guides (for example NIC and agency style manuals), which were not retrieved here.

## What is Sherman Kent's "Words of Estimative Probability" and why does it matter?

### Takeaway
Kent's essay (CIA *Studies in Intelligence*, vol. 8 no. 4, 1964) is the origin of tying verbal probability terms to numeric ranges. It was not adopted at the time, but it is the intellectual ancestor of the ICD 203 table and the UK yardstick.

### Cited Findings
- Published in *Studies in Intelligence* vol. 8, no. 4 (the CSI archive URL is "vol-8-no-4"). The PDF shows CIA Historical Review Program release on 22 Sept 1993. — [CIA CSI](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/archives/vol-8-no-4/words-of-estimative-probability); [CIA PDF](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Words-of-Estimative-Probability.pdf)
- The trigger was NIE 29-51 (March 1951) on Yugoslavia, which judged a Soviet-bloc attack a "serious possibility". Kent privately meant odds of about 65:35 in favour of an attack. The Policy Planning Staff read it as much lower odds, and members of the Board of National Estimates privately interpreted it anywhere from about 20:80 to 80:20. — [CIA PDF](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Words-of-Estimative-Probability.pdf)
- Kent's proposed table: certain 100%; almost certain 93% (±6); probable 75% (±12); chances about even 50% (±10); probably not 30% (±10); almost certainly not 7% (±5); impossible 0%. Synonyms he listed include highly likely and virtually certain (almost certain); likely, "we believe" and "we estimate" (probable); and improbable, unlikely and "we doubt" (probably not). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_of_estimative_probability); [CIA PDF](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Words-of-Estimative-Probability.pdf). Conflict: the summary of the CIA PDF gave "almost certainly not ≈5% (±5)" while Wikipedia gives 7% (±5); 7% is consistent with the symmetry of 93% (±6).
- Kent treated "possible" as an unquantified zone that must not be modified ("serious possibility" is ruled out). He warned against stacking "we believe" on top of odds words. He called his opponents "poets", who resisted numeric precision, as opposed to "mathematicians". The effort stalled under institutional inertia, and he went on by "guerrilla" means. — [CIA PDF](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Words-of-Estimative-Probability.pdf)
- Kent's scale was not adopted, and a later attempt by John McLaughlin at CIA to assign probabilities also failed; analysts and consumers reportedly preferred words. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_of_estimative_probability)
- Later research: Mandel & Irwin (2021, *Judgment and Decision Making* 16(2):363–393) found that verbal-only probability terms gave worse sender-receiver agreement than numeric or combined formats. Verbal estimates drifted toward 50% and produced more fifty-fifty "don't know" answers. The authors call verbal-probability schemes "an ineffective means" of communicating probability and recommend numbers. — [Mandel & Irwin 2021](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/20/200930/jdm200930.html)

### Inferences
- ICD 203's seven bands carry out Kent's core idea: fixed words tied to stated numeric ranges, with the 0% and 100% ends excluded. The empirical work by Mandel and others suggests that even this helps only partly unless the numbers are shown to the reader.

### Gaps
- The CIA PDF did not state the venue in its text; vol. 8 no. 4 comes from the CSI URL. The year 1964 is the standard citation, though the essay mentions a November 1965 date (it may have been revised).

## What are the NATO and UK PHIA equivalents?

### Takeaway
The UK PHIA Probability Yardstick (current page published 24 March 2025) uses seven terms with deliberate gaps between bands. NATO AJP-2.1 (Edition B, 2016) uses a simpler five-band scale.

### Cited Findings
- UK PHIA Probability Yardstick (published 24 March 2025):

| Term | Range |
|---|---|
| Remote chance | >0% to ≈5% |
| Highly unlikely | ≈10% to ≈20% |
| Unlikely | ≈25% to ≈35% |
| Realistic possibility | ≈40% to <50% |
| Likely or Probable | ≈55% to ≈75% |
| Highly likely | ≈80% to ≈90% |
| Almost certain | ≈95% to <100% |

  The terms were "informed by academic research" and set to match how an average reader understands them. The Professional Head of Intelligence Assessment (PHIA) owns the yardstick, which is used across the UK intelligence assessment community. — [UK Gov](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)
- The yardstick deliberately leaves gaps (≈5–10%, 20–25%, 35–40%, 50–55%, 75–80%, 90–95%). The page gives no explicit reason for them. — [UK Gov](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)
- PHIA also publishes "Common Analytical Standards" and a Professional Development Framework, which cites the yardstick (2019). — [PHIA Common Analytical Standards](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/phia-common-analytical-standards/phia-common-analytical-standards); [PHIA PDF (2019), via Wikipedia references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_of_estimative_probability)
- NATO scale as reproduced from AJP-2.1 Ed. B v1 (NATO, 2016): Highly likely >90%; Likely 60–90%; Even chance 40–60%; Unlikely 10–40%; Highly unlikely <10%. — [Mandel & Irwin 2021](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/20/200930/jdm200930.html)

### Inferences
- Key differences from ICD 203: the UK scale has gaps between bands and a "realistic possibility" band below 50%, while ICD 203's bands are contiguous with shared boundaries. NATO has only five bands, with no ends near 0% or 100%. Both the UK and the US separate probability from High/Moderate/Low confidence.

### Gaps
- The NATO table could not be confirmed directly. The AJP-2.1 ratification draft I found online was truncated before the processing/analysis section, so the NATO figures come from a peer-reviewed secondary source. Newer NATO editions (for example AJP-2.1 Ed. C) were not checked.
- The UK gov.uk page metadata shows an internal update timestamp of 20 July 2026, while the public date is 24 March 2025. The terms may predate 2025 (an earlier yardstick appears in the 2019 PHIA framework), but no earlier version was compared.
