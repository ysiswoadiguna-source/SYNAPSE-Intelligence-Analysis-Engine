# Structured Analytic Techniques (SATs) for intelligence analysis and scenario work: technique cards, taxonomy, and evidence on effectiveness

## Q1. For each technique: purpose, when to use, core steps, typical output

### Takeaway
The CIA Tradecraft Primer (2009) gives short, step-by-step procedures for most requested techniques: Key Assumptions Check, Quality of Information Check, Indicators/Signposts, Devil's Advocacy, Team A/Team B, High-Impact/Low-Probability, What If?, Red Team, and Alternative Futures (2x2). Premortem and Structured Self-Critique come from Klein (2007) and Heuer/Pherson. Cone of Plausibility and Indicators Validation appear in the Heuer & Pherson 3rd edition table of contents, but I found only partial step detail for them in open sources.

### Cited Findings

**Key Assumptions Check (KAC)** (Primer: diagnostic)
- Purpose: "List and review the key working assumptions on which fundamental judgments rest." When to use: early in a project, and again before judgments are finalized. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer 2009](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Steps: (1) write down the current analytic line; (2) list every premise it needs, stated and unstated; (3) challenge each one: why must it be true, and does it hold under all conditions?; (4) keep only the assumptions that must hold, and note what would undermine each. Output: a refined list of key assumptions, each with the conditions that would break it. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Heuer & Pherson give examples of key assumptions: another country's intentions, and the reliability of sources. — [Notes on Heuer & Pherson (secondary)](https://arielsheen.com/index.php/2021/02/11/notes-on-structured-analytic-techniques-for-intelligence-analysis)

**Quality of Information Check (QIC)** (Primer: diagnostic)
- Purpose: "Evaluates completeness and soundness of available information sources." When to use: continuously, and before any major assessment. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Steps: (1) build a database of sources by type and date, noting each one's strengths and weaknesses; (2) check sources for accuracy and corroboration; (3) re-examine reporting that was previously dismissed, in light of new facts; (4) flag recalled reporting and confirm caveats were applied; (5) rate how much confidence each source deserves. Output: a rated source inventory showing what is known and what is not. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Empirical caution: in an experiment cited by Mandel & Tetlock, analysts' probability judgments derived from Admiralty-code (source reliability and credibility) ratings were unreliable, and agreement fell as the two ratings diverged. — [Mandel & Tetlock 2018, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02640/full)

**Premortem Analysis** (Klein; Heuer & Pherson pair it with Structured Self-Critique)
- Premortem and Structured Self-Critique are used by a small team that has worked on a future-oriented analysis to challenge its own conclusions. Premortem reframes the question from a different perspective. — [Notes on Heuer & Pherson](https://arielsheen.com/index.php/2021/02/11/notes-on-structured-analytic-techniques-for-intelligence-analysis)
- Steps (per Klein 2007, HBR): (1) run it at project start or just before the decision is finalized; (2) assume the judgment or decision has turned out "spectacularly" wrong, and ask what went wrong; (3) each person privately lists reasons for the failure; (4) group the reasons into categories; (5) revise the original judgment. Output: a categorized list of failure causes and the resulting revisions. — [The Mind Collection (secondary, citing Klein 2007 HBR)](https://themindcollection.com/premortem-analysis/)

**Structured Self-Critique** (Heuer & Pherson)
- Analysts answer a set of questions about sources of uncertainty, the analytic processes used, critical assumptions, diagnosticity of evidence, information gaps, and potential deception. — [Notes on Heuer & Pherson](https://arielsheen.com/index.php/2021/02/11/notes-on-structured-analytic-techniques-for-intelligence-analysis)
- Steps: (1) participants switch from advocates to critics (Pherson's "black hat") and are judged on the weaknesses they find; (2) work through the checklist: sources of uncertainty, analytic process, critical assumptions, evidence, information gaps, factors outside our control, expertise, deception; (3) in a constructive phase, decide what to fix or caveat. Output: a list of weaknesses with the remedies or caveats chosen for each. — [The Mind Collection (secondary)](https://themindcollection.com/premortem-analysis/)

**Devil's Advocacy** (Primer: contrarian)
- Purpose: "challenging a single, strongly held view or consensus" by building the best case for an alternative. When to use: critical judgments, a long-held consensus, or entrenched mindsets. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Steps: (1) outline the mainline judgment and its key assumptions; (2) pick the most vulnerable assumptions; (3) review the supporting information for validity, deception, and gaps; (4) highlight evidence that supports an alternative or contradicts the current view; (5) present the findings, and if flaws are major, consider a separate contrarian paper; label the work "Devil's Advocate" so the official view stays distinct. Output: a conclusion that the mainline view holds, needs more work, or must be changed or caveated. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)

**Team A/Team B** (Primer: contrarian)
- Purpose: "Use of separate analytic teams that contrast two (or more) strongly held views or competing hypotheses." When to use: competing views on a major, long-standing, or high-stakes issue. It is resource-intensive. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Steps: (1) identify the competing hypotheses and form one team per view; (2) each team builds its best case, covering evidence, missing information, assumptions, and logic; (3) debate phase: each team presents, challenges the other, and rebuts; (4) an independent jury questions both teams; (5) the jury weighs the arguments and recommends research and collection priorities. Output: parallel papers plus a jury assessment. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)

**Red Team Analysis** (Primer, imaginative). Heuer & Pherson's "Red Hat Analysis" is the analogous individual-analyst technique; see Gaps.
- Purpose: "Models the behavior of an individual or group" by replicating how the adversary would think. When to use: when the risk of mirror-imaging is high, for example with authoritarian leaders, terrorist cells, or groups operating under different codes of behavior. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Steps: (1) build a team that knows the operating environment, the target's personality, and its thinking style, ideally including people with relevant cultural experience; (2) separate the team from normal analysis; (3) members put themselves in the adversary's circumstances and react to stimuli as the target would; (4) develop the first-person questions the adversary would ask; (5) draft memos or policy papers written as the leader or group would write them. Output: first-person memos, usually without caveats and not coordinated as consensus products. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Mirror-imaging means analysts "impute to a foreign actor the same motives, values, or understanding of an issue that they hold." — [The Mind Collection (secondary)](https://themindcollection.com/red-team-analysis/)

**What If? Analysis** (Primer: contrarian)
- Purpose: assume an event (with negative or positive impact) has already happened, and explain how it could come about. When to use: when a strong mindset holds that the event won't happen, or when a judgment rests on limited information or unproven assumptions. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Steps: (1) state the conventional analytic line; (2) assume the event has happened; (3) select triggering events that make it plausible; (4) reason backward from the event, building a chain of argument from logic and evidence that specifies what must happen at each stage; (5) identify one or more plausible pathways and list indicators for each; (6) weigh the consequences and monitor the indicators. Output: pathways, indicators, and consequences that let decision-makers hedge against being wrong. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)

**High-Impact/Low-Probability (HILP) Analysis** (Primer: contrarian)
- Purpose: highlight "a seemingly unlikely event that would have major policy consequences if it happened." When to use: when analysts and policymakers are confident an event is unlikely but have not considered its consequences. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Steps: (1) define the high-impact outcome clearly; (2) devise one or more plausible pathways to it; (3) insert possible triggers, such as a leader's death or an economic shock; (4) brainstorm with analysts who have diverse experience; (5) identify observable indicators for each pathway; (6) identify factors that would deflect the bad outcome or encourage a good one. Output: mapped pathways, triggers, indicators, and deflecting factors. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)

**Indicators / Signposts of Change** (Primer: diagnostic)
- Purpose: "Periodically review a list of observable events or trends" to spot emerging trends and avoid surprise. Steps: (1) identify the competing hypotheses or scenarios; (2) list the events each one would produce; (3) review and update the lists regularly; (4) judge which hypothesis is most likely from how many of its indicators have changed. Output: an indicator list or matrix, sometimes with trigger thresholds. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Alternative Futures analysis ends with signposts for each future (see below). — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)

**Indicators Validation (diagnosticity)**
- Heuer & Pherson's "Indicator Validator" assesses how diagnostic indicators are. The notes add that "developing good indicators is harder than it seems." — [Notes on Heuer & Pherson](https://arielsheen.com/index.php/2021/02/11/notes-on-structured-analytic-techniques-for-intelligence-analysis)
- The 3rd edition has a section titled "Indicators Generation, Validation, and Evaluation" (9.11). — [SAGE / CQ Press book page](https://www.sagepub.com/en-us/cab/structured-analytic-techniques-for-intelligence-analysis/book255432)
- The diagnosticity logic matches ACH's: weight evidence that is inconsistent with some hypotheses, and run a sensitivity check on a few key items. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)

**Alternative Futures / 2x2 scenario matrix** (Primer: imaginative)
- Purpose: "Systematically explores multiple ways a situation can develop when there is high complexity and uncertainty." — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Steps: (1) define the focal issue, using expert interviews; (2) brainstorm the forces that could affect it; (3) select by consensus the two most critical and most uncertain forces and turn them into axes with relevant endpoints; (4) cross the axes to form a 2x2 matrix with four quadrants; (5) write a narrative for each future explaining how it could plausibly arise; (6) develop signposts for each; (7) policymakers test current strategies against each future. Output: four scenario narratives with signposts. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)

**Cone of Plausibility**
- Listed as section 9.5 among the Foresight Techniques in the Heuer & Pherson 3rd edition. — [SAGE / CQ Press](https://www.sagepub.com/en-us/cab/structured-analytic-techniques-for-intelligence-analysis/book255432)
- Futures-practice version: (1) start from a list of critical uncertainties; (2) set the time horizon and the bounds of the possible; (3) for each uncertainty, describe a business-as-usual (baseline) path, one or two plausible alternatives, and a preferable future; (4) plot each scenario by how far it deviates from the probable future. Output: a cone diagram. Strength: it can handle any number of uncertainties. Weakness: it relies on participants' subjective judgment. Best used after scenarios have been written. — [Government of Alberta CoLab methods](https://colab.alberta.ca/Mtds/Look/Pages/Cone.aspx)

**ACH (for context; already covered in the skill)**
- Primer steps: brainstorm hypotheses → list evidence → build a matrix and mark each item consistent, inconsistent, or N/A → refine → tally inconsistencies → test sensitivity to key evidence → ask what evidence is missing and whether deception is possible → report all hypotheses with relative likelihoods → monitor indicators. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)

### Inferences
- For an AI skill, the cheapest techniques with the most leverage are Premortem, Structured Self-Critique, and KAC/QIC as a final-check pass. HILP and What If? share one engine: assume the outcome, then backcast pathways, triggers, and indicators. They could be implemented as a single "backcasting" routine with two framings.
- Red Team/Red Hat output works best as an explicitly labeled, uncaveated first-person memo kept separate from the main assessment, which is how the Primer specifies it.

- I found no open primary source for the exact Heuer & Pherson steps for **Red Hat Analysis** or for the 3rd edition **Cone of Plausibility** procedure (which drivers and assumptions to state, baseline, alternative scenarios, wildcard). My understanding is that the Cone of Plausibility originates with Charles W. Taylor (US Army War College, c. 1990) and that Heuer & Pherson renamed red-team role-play by individual analysts "Red Hat Analysis." **Neither claim was verified in this session.**
- I did not locate the Indicator Validator's exact scoring scheme (rating each indicator's likelihood under each scenario). It is not verified from a primary text.
- The premortem and self-critique steps come from secondary summaries of Klein (2007, HBR) and Pherson's Handbook. I did not read the primary texts.

## Q2. Which sources categorize the techniques?

### Takeaway
The CIA Tradecraft Primer (2009) uses three categories: Diagnostic, Contrarian, and Imaginative. Heuer & Pherson's 3rd edition (CQ Press; dated Dec 2019, commonly cited as 2020) covers 66 techniques in "six families" (Getting Organized, Exploration, Diagnostic, Reframing, Foresight, Decision Support). The 2nd edition used eight categories.

### Cited Findings
- Primer categories: **Diagnostic** (KAC, QIC, Indicators/Signposts, ACH) make arguments, assumptions, and gaps transparent. **Contrarian** (Devil's Advocacy, Team A/Team B, HILP, What If?) challenge current thinking. **Imaginative** (Brainstorming, Outside-In Thinking, Red Team, Alternative Futures) generate new insights and alternative outcomes. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Primer timeline: at the start, Brainstorming, KAC, Outside-In, What If?, HILP; throughout, ACH, Indicators, Alternative Futures; at the final check, KAC, Devil's Advocacy, Indicators, Deception Detection. The extracted table was partly garbled. — [CIA Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/955180a45afe3f5013772c313b16face/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)
- Heuer & Pherson, 3rd edition: 66 techniques, 9 of them new. Chapter 3 is "The Six Families." Technique chapters are 5 Getting Organized, 6 Exploration, 7 Diagnostic (KAC, ACH, deception detection), 8 Reframing (cause and effect, challenge analysis, conflict management), 9 Foresight (scenarios, Cone of Plausibility 9.5, morphological analysis, Indicators Generation, Validation, and Evaluation 9.11), and 10 Decision Support. 384 pages, CQ Press, listed Dec 2019. — [SAGE / CQ Press](https://www.sagepub.com/en-us/cab/structured-analytic-techniques-for-intelligence-analysis/book255432)
- An earlier edition (apparently the 2nd) used eight categories: Decomposition & Visualization, Idea Generation, Scenarios & Indicators, Hypothesis Generation & Testing, Cause & Effect, Challenge Analysis (which includes Premortem and Structured Self-Critique), Conflict Management, and Decision Support. — [Notes on Heuer & Pherson](https://arielsheen.com/index.php/2021/02/11/notes-on-structured-analytic-techniques-for-intelligence-analysis)

### Inferences
- In the 3rd edition, Premortem, Structured Self-Critique, What If?, HILP, Devil's Advocacy, Red Hat, and Team A/B most likely sit in the Reframing family, and the scenario and indicator tools in Foresight. This is inferred from the chapter descriptions, not from a verified table of contents.

- I could not verify which chapter each requested technique sits in for the 3rd edition, because the publisher page does not list Premortem, Self-Critique, or Red Hat.

## Q3. What does empirical research say about ACH and SATs, and which mitigations are recommended?

### Takeaway
SATs spread on face validity, not evidence. Randomized trials with analysts found that ACH did not improve accuracy and may reduce coherence and consistency. Critics (Chang, Berdini, Mandel, Tetlock) argue that SATs treat bias as one-directional, ignore noise, and lack metrics for overcorrection. The best-supported mitigations are statistical and practice-based: recalibration or coherentizing, aggregating several independent judgments, outside-view base rates, numeric granular probabilities, Brier-scored accuracy tracking, and short probabilistic-reasoning training.

### Cited Findings
- **Mandel, Karvetski & Dhami (2018), JDM 13(6):607–621.** 50 UK analysts were randomized to ACH or control on a four-hypothesis Bayesian task with 12 cues. "ACH failed to improve accuracy": MAE was 0.26 for ACH versus 0.21 for control (p = .14, d = 0.43). Additivity violations were larger under ACH (0.82 vs 0.56, p = .08). The four probabilities summed to 1.77 (ACH) and 1.54 (control). — [JDM 2018](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/18/18803/jdm18803.html)
- In the same study, coherentizing judgments cut MAE from 0.23 to 0.15 (−35%). Equal-weight averaging of the 50 coherentized judgments reached MAE 0.09 (−61% from raw). Averaging as few as about 3 analysts gave substantial gains, and coherence-weighting did not beat equal weighting. — [JDM 2018](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/18/18803/jdm18803.html)
- **Dhami, Belton & Mandel (2019), Applied Cognitive Psychology 33(6):1080–1090.** 50 analysts were randomized. ACH users did not follow all the steps, the evidence that ACH reduces confirmation bias was mixed, and ACH "may increase judgment inconsistency and error." The authors suggest the IC should consider when ACH is useful and explore alternatives. — [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/69049/)
- A later two-study paper (Cognitive Research: Principles & Implications, 2024; full citation not retrieved) notes SATs are "rarely empirically evaluated and/or evidence-based" and are accepted on "face validity." It cites Maegherman et al. (2020): among 191 law students, ACH showed no difference from control, and only about a third of the ACH group used the matrix. In its own Study 1 (n=161), the ACH-style matrix layout did not improve choice or sensitivity to source credibility. In Study 2 (62 Dutch military analysts), 66% naturally used a balanced for/against strategy, which suggests the confirmation-bias premise behind ACH may be overstated. — [Cogn. Research 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-024-00560-y)
- **Mandel & Tetlock (2018), "Correcting Judgment Correctives in National Security Intelligence," Frontiers in Psychology 9:2640.** SATs spread "not because there is evidence it works" but because of endorsement, which the authors call a "goodness heuristic." Bias is bipolar, so warnings about overconfidence can produce underconfidence, and warnings about confirmation bias can produce "analysis paralysis." SATs are "covert greenhouses for noise production." ACH's consistency rating is ill-defined and can amount to the representativeness heuristic. ACH-trained analysts were more prone to coherence-violating unpacking effects. — [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02640/full)
- The same paper recommends: rigorous outside experiments and tournaments; recalibration (extremizing, coherence-based); aggregation ("a highly effective method of error cancelation"), with possible performance-weighting; monitoring the accuracy of the IC's own products; and cost-benefit arguments for research funding. — [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02640/full)
- **Chang & Berdini (with Mandel & Tetlock supervising, IARPA-funded), "Restructuring Structured Analytic Techniques in Intelligence," Intelligence and National Security 33 (2018): 337–356.** Volume and pages are as cited in Mandel & Tetlock 2018. The paper questions whether SATs improve analysis given that there are no reliable metrics to detect "overcorrection." It won the 2017 Inman Award. — [UT Austin Intelligence Studies Project](https://intelligencestudies.utexas.edu/?p=604); citation details per [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02640/full)
- **Tetlock / Good Judgment Project practices.** A roughly one-hour training module improved accuracy, with effects lasting at least a year. Teaming, more frequent updating, and longer deliberation also helped (Mellers et al. 2015). Forecasts tagged as using "comparison classes," i.e. the outside view or base rates, averaged a Brier score of 0.17, versus 0.26 for the next-best tag (Chang et al. 2016). Superforecasters were calibrated to within about 0.01. About 70% kept superforecaster status year to year, and the year-to-year performance correlation was 0.65. GJP aggregates beat the control group by more than 60% and an IC prediction market by 25–30%. Extremizing remains debated. — [AI Impacts summary of GJP evidence (secondary)](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)
- **Granularity.** A claim that rounding superforecasters' forecasts to 0.05 hurts accuracy (Friedman et al. 2018) is reported with an internal correction to 0.1, so treat the exact threshold as unreliable. — [AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)
- The Brier score is a measure of inaccuracy: higher means worse. — [AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)

### Inferences
- For an AI analysis skill: (a) state numeric probabilities that sum to 1 across exhaustive hypotheses, and renormalize, since coherentizing gave a 35% gain; (b) start every estimate from a base rate or reference class (the outside view), then adjust; (c) generate several independent estimates, for example multiple passes or personas, and average them, since gains were large at about 3; (d) treat ACH as a structuring and transparency tool rather than an accuracy engine, and define "consistency" explicitly as P(evidence | hypothesis), i.e. a likelihood ratio, to avoid the representativeness heuristic; (e) log forecasts with resolution criteria so they can be Brier-scored later; (f) watch for overcorrection, such as underconfidence or paralysis, after debiasing prompts.
- Note on study scope: the core ACH null results come from small samples (n≈50) on simplified tasks, so they show a lack of demonstrated benefit rather than proven harm.

- I did not retrieve exact effect sizes for Mellers et al. (2015) training, i.e. the percent Brier improvement from CHAMPS KNOW, or primary texts for Friedman et al. (2018) on granularity.
- I found no controlled empirical tests of Premortem, Devil's Advocacy, Team A/B, Red Hat, or Cone of Plausibility in an intelligence setting in this session.
